# Release of Bgee version 16.0

We are happy to announce the release of Bgee version 16.0. This represents a major update of our infrastructure for scaling-up the integration of single-cell data, with immediate integration of millions of cells in 16 species, paving the way for even larger-scale projects in the future.

**Highlights**: a major increase in both single-cell and bulk data; improved method for detection of expression signals; an original tool to compare expression across genes and species, up to the single-cell resolution; and updated genomes and gene orthology mappings.

### New data

This release is characterised by a major increase in single-cell data, in 16 species total. We have integrated 1,267 new libraries (40 experiments, 13 new species with single-cell data, about 5.8 million cells) of droplet-based single-cell RNA-Seq (e.g., 10x Genomics). This includes a large experiment of mouse brain ([SRP467631](/experiment/SRP467631), 4.0 million cells). We have also integrated 44,259 cells (34 experiments, 9 species) of full-length single-cell RNA-Seq (e.g., Smart-Seq2).

In addition, **30,057 bulk RNA-Seq libraries** are now used in expression calls, up from 16,132 in Bgee 15.2. This corresponds to 596 experiments, and includes large increases in the following species: human (7,327 libraries), mouse (3,490 libraries), pig (2,688 libraries), cattle (2,473 libraries), fly (1,285 libraries), rat (1,283 libraries), sheep (1,136 libraries), salmon (1,007 libraries), rhesus macaque (987 libraries), dog (582 libraries), and crab-eating macaque (440 libraries).

Finally, we integrate from Model Organism Databases **82,359 _in situ_ hybridization experiments** for 40,920 genes.

In this release, we are discontinuing the integration of Affymetrix and EST data. The data and integrated calls from Bgee 15.2 (12,996 Affymetrix chips, 7 species; 3,262 EST libraries, 5 species) are still accessible through our [archive site](https://archives.bgee.org/15-2/).

### Expression calls

We have implemented a new approach to call gene expression present or absent. It is based on a new in-house method providing p-values of active expression ([Brandulas Cammarata _et al_](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.03.31.486555)). Notably, you will see **many more significant absent calls**, i.e., we have data, and are confident that the gene is not expressed in this condition.

Related, a new way of combining p-values replaces the FDR correction:

- The p-values of biological replicates (samples annotated to the same condition) are averaged with a weighted arithmetic mean;
- They are then propagated up the graph of conditions with a weighted harmonic mean, so that strong expression in one sub-structure (e.g., the midbrain) supports expression in the parent structure (e.g., the brain).
- Calls now report a p-value instead of an FDR. The quality thresholds remain gold for p ≤ 0.01, silver for p ≤ 0.05.

Single-cell data, pseudo-bulked per library and cell type, contribute to present calls. Absent calls rely on bulk RNA-Seq and _in situ_ data only.

### Website and tools

- The new [Expression graph](/search/expression-matrix) replaces the Expression comparison page. It provides visualization to easily generate presentation-ready figures of gene expression comparisons across genes and species, from tissues and organs, up to single-cell resolution. It can compare a list of genes across species, matching anatomical entities through homology, and can add the orthologs and paralogs of a gene. Graphs export as SVG or PNG, data as TSV.
- Expression calls can now be downloaded from the gene page.
- Single-cell H5AD metadata have been updated to follow the scFAIR standard, that is adopted by major single-cell resources, such as CELLxGENE, Bgee, or FENIX Explorer.
- We have updated our expression enrichment analysis tool [TopAnat](/analysis/top-anat). The default background is now the protein-coding genes expressed in at least one condition, instead of all genes with data. This is to minimize bias for the most frequent case, where users only input protein-coding genes. Users can still define any background of their choice, including non-coding genes. The web form runs only a Fisher test without decorrelation, which is much faster. Options of decorrelation remain available in our R Bioconductor package [BgeeDB](https://bioconductor.org/packages/BgeeDB/). And we have refactored our packages and webtools to make the analyses fully consistent across platforms.

### Programmatic access

- The FTP has [expression call files](/download/gene-expression-calls) for all 52 species: simple and advanced, for anatomical entities and for all conditions. It also has [processed expression values](/download/processed-expression-values) (H5AD files for single-cell data), cross-references, gene summaries, as well as the [easyBgee dump](/download/data-dumps) (source for our RDF triples used in SPARQL endpoint), and its RDF export.
- The REST API serves the new calls, and the expression comparison endpoint has been removed. Our [SPARQL endpoint](/sparql-editor/) has been updated accordingly.
- It is necessary to update the [BgeeDB R package](https://bioconductor.org/packages/BgeeDB/) to access Bgee 16.0.

### Genomes

Genomes were updated from Ensembl 102 to Ensembl 113 and from Ensembl Metazoa 49 to Ensembl Metazoa 60, leading to a new genome assembly or assembly version for 22 species. These include mouse GRCm39, rat mRatBN7.2, chicken GRCg7b, dog ROS_Cfam_1.0, salmon Ssal_v3.1 and _X. laevis_ v10.1. In total Bgee 16.0 contains 1,701,684 genes, vs 1,559,014 in Bgee 15.2.

Orthology data has been updated to [OMA](https://omabrowser.org/) release May 2026.

### Ontologies

The anatomy, cell type, and developmental and life stage ontologies are the same as in Bgee 15.2.
